Four dates sit in one two-page notice about Gamcheon Culture Village. They are 1 August 2026, 1 January 2027, 31 December 2027 and 1 January 2028. Each attaches to a different rule. Only the first has arrived, and it is doing two jobs at once.

The notice went up on the village's own board on 31 July 2026. Its body is empty. The content is two JPEG images, and the images are bilingual — proper English, written by the district, not machine output. So the information does exist in English. It exists as pixels, inside a picture, on a notice board, on a website whose English button turns out to be a Google Translate widget. Translation software cannot read a picture. Neither can a search engine. We have written before about [official English pages in Korea drifting away from the Korean ones](/en/now/english-pages-korea-out-of-date/); this is a variant we had not seen — English that was written, and then buried.

That is why you have probably not read this anywhere.

## What actually changed, and when

On <strong>1 August 2026</strong>, Saha-gu District Office designated Gamcheon Culture Village a Special Management Area — 특별관리지역 *(teuk-byeol gwan-ri ji-yeok)* — under the Tourism Promotion Act and the district's own tourism ordinance.

If that phrase is familiar, it is because we have written about it before. Bukchon in Seoul was designated under the same law in July 2024, and [visiting one Bukchon lane outside 10:00–17:00 now costs ₩100,000](/en/guides/bukchon-hanok-village-visiting-hours-fine/). The instinct is to assume Gamcheon works the same way. It does not, and the differences matter more than the similarity.

| | Bukchon | Gamcheon |
|---|---|---|
| Designated | 1 July 2024 | 1 August 2026 |
| Guidance period | 1 Nov 2024 – 28 Feb 2025 | 1 Aug 2026 – 31 Dec 2027 |
| Fine begins | 1 March 2025 | 1 January 2028 |
| Fine amount | ₩100,000 | Not published |
| What triggers it | Photographing in the lane out of hours | Driving a tourist vehicle on one road |

Read the bottom row twice. In Bukchon the restricted act is <strong>lifting your phone</strong>. In Gamcheon, on the evidence of the district's own notice, the act that carries the fine is <strong>arriving by car or taxi</strong> on a particular street during the day. Nothing in the notice attaches a penalty to walking, standing, or taking photographs.

Gamcheon's guidance period is also seventeen months long. Bukchon's was four. Whatever else that means, it means the district is in no hurry.

## The one rule that is already in force

Three of the four dates are in the future. One is not.

Since <strong>1 August 2026</strong>, organised groups of ten or more are required to buy the official visitor map. That is printed on the notice as a live measure, with its own effective date in brackets, while the designation as a whole takes effect on 1 January 2027.

If you are travelling as a couple or a family this does not touch you. If you are a tour leader, a school, or a group booking through an agency, it does, and it did over a month ago.

There is a problem with telling you what it costs. <strong>Nobody publishes the price.</strong> The village's official shop sells seven things online — a mood lamp at ₩19,000, a handkerchief at ₩8,000, a jigsaw at ₩6,000, building blocks at ₩29,000, the moon-shaped bread at ₩13,000, kiln salt at ₩4,000, seaweed snacks at ₩3,500 — and the map is not among them. English guides quote ₩1,000 and ₩2,000 with equal confidence. We are not going to pick one. Bring small cash and ask at the information centre, which is where the map is sold.

The restricted stretch is about one kilometre of 감내2로 *(Gam-nae 2-ro)*, running from the information centre entrance to the photo zone the district calls Memories' Shadow. The hours are 09:00 to 18:00. The vehicles named are tourist vehicles, and the notice puts taxis inside the brackets explicitly: 관광객 탑승차량(택시포함).

That last detail is the one to carry with you. Gamnae 2-ro is the village's address — the information centre is at 감내2로 203. A taxi is the obvious way up a very steep hill, and it is the thing being restricted.

Two more measures sit alongside it. Tour buses will need an advance reservation, and the district says it will step up enforcement against illegal parking, littering, noise, and behaviour that intrudes on residents' privacy.

Up to ten million won is a ceiling, not a price. The actual figure comes from the district ordinance, and this is where it gets interesting. We read the Saha-gu Tourism Promotion Ordinance in full on the national law database — the version in force, last amended on 14 April 2025. It is short. It sets up a committee, defines the committee's remit, and lists "designation, amendment and removal of special management areas" as one of the things that committee advises on.

<strong>The word 과태료 *(gwa-tae-ryo)*, administrative fine, does not appear in it anywhere.</strong>

So the ordinance that the designation notice cites as its legal basis does not, in its published form, contain the penalty the notice promises. That is not a scandal — it is almost certainly why the guidance period runs to the end of 2027. There is an ordinance amendment to write, and seventeen months in which to write it. But it does mean that anyone quoting you a Gamcheon fine figure today is quoting something that has not been enacted. Bukchon's ₩100,000 comes from Jongno's ordinance and applies to Jongno.

## Hours: two different clocks

Now the part that trips people up on an ordinary visit.

The village is a public hillside of public streets. It has no gate and no ticket. Busan's official tourism site lists admission as free, and lists the village as open every day of the year, with these hours:

| | Opening hours |
|---|---|
| March – October | 09:00 – 18:00 |
| November – February | 09:00 – 17:00 |
| Closed | Never |

Those are attraction hours — in practice, when the information centre and the village-run shops and cafés are working. They are not the same instrument as the Special Management Area's visitor hours, which apply from 2027 to the designated residential zone only and are 09:00–18:00 without a seasonal change.

The gap is real and it is small: in winter, the tourism board's hours end at 17:00 while the residential-access window in the designation runs to 18:00. Neither closes the streets. What ends at 17:00 in December is the desk that sells you the map, not the hill.

A third clock, unrelated to either: the craft workshops run 09:00–16:00, two hours before the information centre in summer. If you came for a pottery or dyeing session, you are on the earliest of the three schedules. The resident artists' studios ask you to call ahead.

## Getting there without a car

Given the direction the traffic rules are moving, this is the section that ages best.

Gamcheon sits high, and every route ends with a 마을버스 *(ma-eul-beo-seu)*, a small local village bus. From the metro:

| From | Village bus | Get off |
|---|---|---|
| Goejeong Station, exit 6 | Saha 1, Saha 1-1 | Gamjeong Elementary School, then 5 min on foot |
| Toseong Station, exit 6 | Saha 1-1, Seo-gu 2, Seo-gu 2-2 | Gamjeong Elementary School, then 5 min on foot |

From Busan Station the village's own page gives Seo-gu 2-2 directly, which is the single simplest connection if you have just come off the [KTX from Seoul](/en/now/seoul-to-busan-ktx-srt-integration-2026/). From Haeundae, take the express 3006 from the Uncheon stop to Gamcheon Sageori and change to a village bus. From Gimhae Airport, the light rail to Sasang, then bus 161 to Sampung Apartment, then the village bus.

One small trap in the official text itself. The same page writes the same route two ways — 사하1 in one section and 사하구1 in another. Typing the wrong one into a map app returns nothing. If a search fails, try it without the 구.

The village's own directions page ends with a line worth taking literally: there is not enough parking, please use public transport. That request was written before the traffic restriction. It reads differently now.

## What is up there

The district publishes three walking routes with honest timings — the main road there and back at about 40 minutes, and two longer loops at about an hour and twenty. The stamp route is the long one, and the stamps are the reason the map exists.

The village was built by people who had nowhere else to go: followers of the Taegeukdo faith and refugees from the Korean War, who terraced a hillside in the 1950s into the stepped rows that are now the view. The art came much later, in 2009, through a national village art project. Both facts are the district's, and the second one explains why the alleys are full of sculpture and the houses are not.

Among the facilities: a small museum of objects the residents themselves collected, the Haneulmaru observatory built inside a preserved resident's house, a bakery, a village café whose profits go back into resident welfare, and a gift shop named after the Little Prince's asteroid. The information centre also stores luggage, which is not obvious and is useful on a hill.

One entry on that list is marked 현재 미운영 — currently not operating. It is 감내맛집, the village-run snack restaurant. It still appears as a stop on all three of the district's recommended walking routes, on a page last updated in February 2025. If you were planning to eat there, plan again.

None of that is enforced with a fine. It is the reason the fine is coming.

## What this article does not claim

We have not been to Gamcheon. Everything here is read off the district's, the operator's and the law database's pages on 9 September 2026.

We do not know what the fine will be — the published ordinance has no penalty article and we have not seen a draft amendment. We do not know the price of the map, so we have not repeated either of the two figures circulating in English.

Three of these four dates have not yet arrived, and a designation notice is a district office's intent, not a guarantee. If you are reading this after 1 January 2027, check the village notice board.
