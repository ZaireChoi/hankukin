₩1,150. ₩2,000. ₩3,000. Three numbers cover almost every bus ride on Jeju, and which one you pay is written on the front of the bus before you board — not in the fare, in the **route number**.

Almost everything published in English about Jeju quietly assumes a rental car. The island does have a bus network, and it is legible in a way Seoul's is not: the numbers are grouped so that the first digit of a route number states the route's job. Learn eight prefixes and you can read a departure board you have never seen before.

<ChartJejuBusNumbers caption="Read the number, not the destination sign. Only routes beginning with 1 — and the 600/800 airport limousines — charge by distance. Everything else on the island is one flat fare." />

## Eight kinds of route, and only one is priced by distance

A bus numbered 111 is a 급행버스 *(geup-haeng beo-seu)*, a rapid bus: long highway runs between towns, priced by how far you ride. A bus numbered 201, 345, 625 or 781 is a trunk or branch route, and every one of them costs the same ₩1,150 no matter how long you sit on it. The 810 and 820 tourist circular routes are charged as branch routes too.

That is the whole system, and it means the practical question on Jeju is never "how far is it" but "am I on a 1 or not".

## The habit that charges you the maximum

Here is the part no English guide mentions, because it is a footnote in Korean on the operator's own fare page.

A rapid bus paid by card is ₩2,000 for the first 20 km, then ₩200 more per additional 5 km, capping at ₩3,000 past 40 km. Paid in cash it is ₩3,000. Not ₩3,000 for a long ride — ₩3,000 for **any** ride. A short hop that would have cost ₩2,000 on a card costs half as much again in coins, and the machine gives you no signal that it happened.

This is not a new rule anyone forgot to translate. The operator's notice that set the rapid-bus fare at ₩2,000–₩3,000 with a ₩200 step per 5 km took effect on 30 December 2017, and the numbers on the live fare page in August 2026 match that notice line for line — base, step, cap and the youth and child rates. Eight years and eight months, no change. That is worth saying plainly, because it is not what we usually find: nearly every other Korean fare table we track has moved.

The prepaid transit cards you already have work here. The operator lists T-money *(티머니)*, Cashbee, Mybi, Nail Plus, Hanpay and One Pass — the nationwide-compatible cards sold in any convenience store. If you have been [using a card in Seoul](/en/now/seoul-transport-fares-subway-bus-taxi/), it works on Jeju.

## What a ride costs

| Route type | Adult, card | Adult, cash |
|---|---|---|
| Trunk and branch routes (2–7), tourist circular 810/820 | ₩1,150 | ₩1,200 |
| Rapid bus (1xx), first 20 km | ₩2,000 | ₩3,000 |
| Rapid bus, each further 5 km | +₩200 | — |
| Rapid bus, over 40 km | ₩3,000 | ₩3,000 |
| Village bus — Dongbok-ri, and the **Chuja** and **Udo** island services | ₩950 | ₩1,000 |

The airport limousines are priced by distance like a rapid bus: the 600, which runs the airport to Jungmun and on to the Seogwipo KAL Hotel, is listed at ₩1,300 to ₩5,500 for an adult; the 800 and 800-1, which run the Jeju bus terminal to the airport and down to Seogwipo without touching Jungmun, at ₩1,300 to ₩5,000.

Youth (13–18) and child (6–12) fares are lower — ₩850 and ₩350 on the flat routes — but a prepaid card only charges the reduced rate if the card has been registered as a youth or child card on the card company's site. An unregistered card charges your teenager the adult fare.

## Tag off, or the transfer did not happen

Jeju gives you free or discounted transfers, and the rule has two conditions in it that people miss.

The clock starts when you tag off, not when you get on — so if you walk past the reader on your way out of the back door, there is no transfer to discount. Then: if the next bus costs the same or less, it is free; if it costs more, you pay only the difference. Trunk to branch, or branch to trunk, is free — they are the same fare. Trunk to rapid is ₩850 instead of ₩2,000, though the rapid bus still adds its distance charge when you tag off at the end.

Two traps sit inside that.

**The same number both ways does not count.** The operator's own example is 111 and 112 — different numbers on the LED, so the transfer applies. Ride 111 out and 111 back and it does not, even hours apart. For visitors this bites hardest on the tourist circular routes, where 810-1 and 810-2 are the same loop in opposite directions: the operator states explicitly that a shared three-digit stem means no transfer discount between them.

**And one card does not cover two people.**

<Original
  ko="다인승 승차는 허용하나, 다인승 환승은 불가"

Couples tap one card twice out of habit. It works for boarding; the discount on the connecting bus is then applied to one person only. Two cards, not one.

## The airport is not the terminal

Most rapid routes start at 제주공항 *(Jeju gong-hang)*, the airport: 101 down the east coast road to Namwon, 111 to Seongsan Port on the eastern tip, 121 and 122 to Pyoseon, 131 and 132 to Namwon by the inland Namjo road, and 181 and 182 to Seogwipo and back — 181 crossing on the 5·16 road and returning by the Pyeonghwa road, 182 doing it the other way round.

Two are not at the airport. **102**, the west-coast rapid, and **151**, the inland run to Moseulpo, are both listed from 제주버스터미널 *(Jeju bus terminal)*. If you land intending to go west, that is a leg you have to plan rather than discover.

The limousines fill that gap in one direction only. The 800 starts at the bus terminal and calls at the airport on its way south, so it will pick you up; the 600 starts at the airport but goes to Jungmun and Seogwipo, not west. For transfer purposes both count as rapid buses.

## How you actually reach the interior

The thing that takes longest to work out from outside Korea is that Jeju's mid-mountain attractions are not on a through route. They hang off **환승정류장** *(hwan-seung jeong-nyu-jang)*, transfer stops on the highway, and the pattern is always the same: rapid bus down the trunk road, get off at the transfer stop, change to a local loop.

The two tourist circular routes are exactly this. 810-1 and 810-2 loop out of Daecheon transfer stop past Geoseunsaemi Oreum, the Bijarim cedar forest and Seonheul; 820-1 and 820-2 loop out of Donggwang transfer stop past the automobile museum and Shinhwa History Park. Both are charged as flat-fare routes, so with a card tagged off inside 40 minutes the change onto them is free.

Which is the honest summary of Jeju without a car: it is not that the buses do not go there. It is that getting there is two buses and a transfer stop, and nobody tells you that in English before you land.

## Where our checking stopped

We read fares, route classes and transfer rules off the operator's own pages on 20 August 2026, and quoted the Korean above so you can check us. We did **not** obtain first and last bus times, journey times, or how far any stop is from the attraction it is named after — those sit in per-route timetables we could not open cleanly, and guessing at them is exactly how a reader ends up stranded at a rural stop at dusk.
