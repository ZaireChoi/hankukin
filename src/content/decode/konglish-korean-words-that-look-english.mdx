A small plate lands on your table that nobody ordered. Fried dumplings, maybe, or a bowl of egg soup. You start reaching for the menu to work out what it will cost, and the person who put it down says one word: **서비스** *(seo-bi-seu)*.

It is free. That is what the word means here.

Korean has taken in several hundred English words, and most of them behave. 커피 *(keo-pi)* is coffee. 버스 *(beo-seu)* is a bus. The trouble is the handful that came through the door and were given a different job — because those are exactly the ones you will read confidently, and be wrong about.

## The word that means the opposite of a bill

The national dictionary gives 서비스 four senses. The first two are the ones you expect: provision of labour, and looking after someone. The third is the one that matters at a table.

The dictionary's own example sentence is a desk thrown in with a computer. So when the word arrives attached to food, it is not a description of the establishment's attentiveness. It is a statement about your bill. The dish is not on it.

Two words that sit near it are worth separating from it carefully, because they look like the same promise and are not.

**셀프** *(sel-peu)* is defined as a way of doing things in restaurants and shops where the customer performs part of the service themselves. That is an instruction, not an offer. At a counter marked 셀프 — water, cutlery, side dishes, trays — nobody is coming to bring it to you, and standing there waiting is the mistake.

**리필** *(ri-pil)* is the refilling of an emptied container, or the service of doing so. Note what that definition does not contain: any mention of free. Whether a refill costs money is the shop's business, and 서비스 is the word that would have told you it did not.

If you would like to be able to ask for these out loud, the [phrases that carry an ordinary Korean meal](/en/decode/korean-phrases-shops-restaurants/) are the ones to learn first.

## The words that decide where you sleep

This is where the money stops being small. These all appear in Korean accommodation and rental listings, and each is borrowed from an English word that points somewhere else.

**펜션** *(pen-syeon)* has nothing to do with retirement.

Note what the definition puts on each side of the scale: the *atmosphere* of a 민박 *(min-bak)*, a family-run guest room, and the *convenience* of a hotel. It is a self-contained holiday cottage, usually outside a city, usually rented whole. A hotel front desk is not part of the definition, and neither is anyone being there when you arrive.

**오피스텔** *(o-pi-seu-tel)* is a contraction of "office hotel", and the dictionary is blunt about which half wins. The head noun of the definition is 사무실 *(sa-mu-sil)*, office; the living quarters are the modifier hanging off it. An office fitted with simple residential facilities, serving both functions at once — in that order.

**원룸** *(won-rum)* is "one-room" — a layout where bedroom, living room, kitchen and dining room are the same room. That is a studio flat, and it is a description of the floor plan rather than of the standard.

For completeness, **아파트** *(a-pa-teu)* is not the unit you rent. The dictionary defines it as a form of collective housing: a building of five or more storeys divided floor by floor into separate households. Korean uses it for the block, not the flat.

## One word that decides what is in the glass

**사이다** *(sa-i-da)* is listed twice, and the order tells you something.

Sense 「1」 is a soft drink: sugar water with sodium carbonate and flavouring, sweet and cold. Sense 「2」 is fermented apple juice — a strong drink, for which the dictionary gives the synonym 사과주 *(sa-gwa-ju)*.

So the English meaning is in there. It is just second, behind a sweet fizzy drink that shares the word — which means 사이다 by itself does not tell you which of the two is coming. If it is the alcohol you are after, the dictionary hands you the word that only points at it: 사과주.

## The words that get you helped

These cost nothing to get wrong, but knowing them is the difference between being understood in one go and miming.

| Word | Say it | The dictionary's sense |
|---|---|---|
| 콘센트 | *kon-sen-teu* | A wall socket — the thing you put a plug into. From "concentric plug" |
| 노트북 | *no-teu-buk* | A laptop computer, not a paper notebook |
| 핸드폰 | *haen-deu-pon* | The everyday word for a mobile phone |
| 비닐 | *bi-nil* | Plastic, in the sheet-and-bag sense. A 비닐봉투 *(bi-nil-bong-tu)* is a carrier bag |
| 마트 | *ma-teu* | A large discount store or a fairly large retail shop |
| 헬스 | *hel-seu* | Training with equipment, usually at a 헬스클럽 *(hel-seu-keul-leop)*. A gym, not health |

핸드폰 is worth having ready for an unhappier reason: it is the word you will need if you have to [report something left on the subway](/en/now/lost-something-in-korea-what-to-do/).

## 미팅 is not a meeting

If a Korean student invites you to a 미팅 *(mi-ting)*, the dictionary is very specific about what has been proposed: a word used mainly by students, for a gathering held in a group by male and female students for the purpose of socialising.

It is a group blind date. The word for the kind of meeting you take notes at is 회의 *(hoe-ui)*.

## Four words the dictionary does not have

Here is the part we did not expect to find. We searched the Standard Korean Dictionary — the state-edited one — for the loanwords a visitor runs into at a counter, and got nothing back at all.

| Searched on 23 August 2026 | Results in the Standard Korean Dictionary |
|---|---|
| 리필 *(ri-pil)* | 0 |
| 스킨 *(seu-kin)* | 0 |
| 원샷 *(won-syat)* | 0 |
| 키오스크 *(ki-o-seu-keu)* | 0 |
| 셀프 *(sel-peu)* | 1 — a philosophy term meaning the self |

The single 셀프 entry is not the counter sign. It is 자아 *(ja-a)*, the unified consciousness that persists through changing experience. Useful, but not for finding the water.

All four of the missing words do appear in 우리말샘 *(u-ri-mal-saem)*, the National Institute's open dictionary, which anyone may contribute to and which specialists review. That is where **스킨** turns out to be a clear liquid that puts moisture into the skin and smooths its surface — in other words, toner, which is worth knowing before you spend an hour in [an Olive Young in Myeongdong](/en/now/olive-young-myeongdong-which-store/) looking for a bottle labelled in English. It is where **원샷** means emptying a glass in one go, **리필** means refilling an empty container *or the service of doing so*, and **키오스크** is an unattended information-and-service terminal of the kind installed in stations, public offices, hospitals and museums.

Several of these entries carry a line we have not seen in an English dictionary: 규범 표기는 미확정이다 *(gyu-beom pyo-gi-neun mi-hwak-jeong-i-da)* — the standard spelling has not been settled. The institute is telling you, in the entry itself, that the word arrived faster than the rule for writing it.

The lesson is not about lexicography. It is that a shop counter moves faster than a national dictionary, so the vocabulary printed above a till is the vocabulary least likely to be in the book — and a phrasebook assembled from the standard dictionary will be thinnest exactly where you are standing.

None of this is hard to survive. It is worth ninety minutes to be able to [sound the letters out for yourself](/en/hangul/learn-to-read-korean-signs-alphabet/) — once you can, a word like 마트 or 노트북 stops being a puzzle and turns back into an English word with a Korean accent. 콘센트 will not, and that is the honest half of this: some of them you simply have to be told.

Just not 서비스. That one you say thank you for.

## What this article does not claim

Every definition above was read on 23 August 2026 in the National Institute of Korean Language's own dictionaries, and each word's entry is linked in the sources. We are reporting what those entries say, not how every Korean speaker uses every word — dictionaries lag ordinary speech, which is the point of the last section. 우리말샘 is an open dictionary with public contributions and specialist review, so it is cited separately from the Standard Korean Dictionary rather than mixed in with it. We have not surveyed prices, and no claim here is about what any particular shop or lodging charges or provides.
