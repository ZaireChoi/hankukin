Behind Changdeokgung there is a valley the kings kept for themselves.

No axis, no grid, no ceremonial approach — just a path that follows the ground as it falls, past a square pond with a round island in it, past a library where a king read, up to a pavilion with its feet in the water. Three hundred years of Joseon monarchs walked out of the throne room and came here to stop being kings for an afternoon.

Roughly a hundred people get in per session. That is the whole story of this page.

**Changdeokgung's Huwon — the Secret Garden — sits outside every deal the palaces offer.** Hanbok does not open it. The combined ticket does not include it. Culture Day does not cover it.

The free-admission list for the Huwon is short and specific: children aged six and under, and Korean nationals holding veterans' or disability status. Hanbok is not on it. Nor is the Culture Day discount on the last Wednesday of the month. Nor is the combined palace ticket. The Huwon sits outside all three.

This is not an oversight on anyone's part. The Huwon is a limited-capacity site, and every mechanism that would increase footfall has been removed from it deliberately.

## What it actually costs

You need **two** tickets, not one.

| | |
|---|---|
| Changdeokgung palace grounds | ₩3,000 |
| Huwon (Secret Garden) | ₩5,000 |
| **Total for an adult** | **₩8,000** |

The palace ticket is not optional. The official guidance is explicit that Huwon visitors must also buy the palace admission ticket — the Huwon entrance is inside Changdeokgung, so you pass through the palace to reach it.

Ages 7 to 18 pay ₩2,500 for the Huwon. Visitors 65 and over pay the full ₩5,000 — the senior discount that applies elsewhere does not apply here.

And the ₩6,000 combined ticket, the one that covers four palaces plus Jongmyo for six months, **excludes the Huwon.** If you bought it expecting the Secret Garden to be included, it is not.

## How the booking works

Two ways in, and they open at different times.

**Online, in advance.** Booking opens at **10:00 KST six days before your visit date** — counting the visit date itself as day zero — and closes the day before. First come, first served.

**On the day, at the palace.** A separate allocation goes on sale at the ticket office from **09:00** on the day of the visit. Also first come, first served.

So there is no single deadline to miss. If the online window is gone, the same-day counter is a second chance.

## The months where this gets hard

The official guidance names them, which is unusual and worth taking seriously:

> **April–May and October–November.** Both the online tickets and the same-day counter tickets sell out very quickly.

Those are the blossom and foliage months, which is to say the months everyone wants the Huwon. If you are visiting Seoul in one of them and the Secret Garden matters to you, treat the 10:00 booking six days out as an appointment rather than an intention. In the other eight months, the same-day counter is usually a reasonable fallback.

## Do not turn up on a Monday

**Changdeokgung closes on Mondays.** ([Gyeongbokgung closes Tuesdays](/en/guides/five-palaces-seoul-which-one/) — the two are opposite, and it catches people out.)

This trips people up because Gyeongbokgung — the palace most itineraries start with — closes on **Tuesdays** instead. Two palaces, two different closing days, and visitors routinely plan around the wrong one.

If a closing day lands on a public holiday, the site opens and the closure moves to the next non-holiday day.

| Season | Changdeokgung hours | Last entry |
|---|---|---|
| Jun–Aug | 09:00–18:30 | 17:30 |
| Feb–May, Sep–Oct | 09:00–18:00 | 17:00 |
| Nov–Jan | 09:00–17:30 | 16:30 |

## So is hanbok pointless here?

No — it just does a different job than you might expect.

Hanbok still gets you into the Changdeokgung palace grounds free, which is the ₩3,000 half of the bill. You would still buy the ₩5,000 Huwon ticket. And the palace grounds themselves include Seongjeonggak, the crown prince's study hall, which is the part of Changdeokgung we find most worth the walk.

The honest framing is this. Hanbok is not a discount scheme; the money involved is small either way. It changes what the day feels like, and it happens to be free at four palaces and a shrine. The Secret Garden is a separate decision, made six days earlier, at ten in the morning.

## What this article does not claim

We have not tested the booking system ourselves, and we are describing the rules as the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center publishes them, checked on the date shown above. Booking windows and prices are the kind of thing that changes without much announcement.

We are also not linking to resellers. The Huwon is booked through the official channel; if you find it offered elsewhere at a markup, that is not us.
