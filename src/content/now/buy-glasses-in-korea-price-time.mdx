Three things about buying glasses in Korea surprise almost every visitor, and the first one saves you a whole appointment.

**You do not go to an eye doctor.** You go to an optician's shop — **안경점 *(an-gyeong-jeom)*** or **안경원 *(an-gyeong-won)*** — and they test your eyes themselves, in the shop, and make the glasses from that test. There is no prescription to obtain first and nothing to book.

**You walk in.** No appointment exists to make. Most shops have you tested, fitted and paying inside about **ten minutes**, and single-vision lenses in a common prescription are often ground while you wait.

**It is cheaper than most places you have come from**, which is the part with numbers attached.

## They test your eyes in the shop — this is the part nobody expects

If you have only ever bought glasses in the US, the UK, Australia or most of Europe, the sequence you know is: **book an eye exam, attend the exam, receive a prescription, then take that prescription somewhere and buy glasses.** Two appointments, sometimes two businesses, often two separate bills.

**In Korea that entire front half does not exist.**

You walk into the optician's. Someone sits you at an autorefractor — the machine you rest your chin on that reads your eyes automatically — then checks the result with a trial frame and a letter chart. **It takes a few minutes and it is normally included in the price of the glasses**, not billed separately.

Then they make the glasses from that test, usually while you wait.

### Why it works this way

Korean opticians (**안경사**, *an-gyeong-sa*) are a **licensed profession** with their own national qualification, and refraction — measuring your prescription — is part of what that licence covers. They are not doctors and they are not diagnosing anything. They are measuring your eyes to grind a lens, which is a technical job and one they are trained and licensed to do.

So there is nothing irregular about it. **It is simply that Korea puts the measurement and the manufacture under one roof**, where a lot of other countries split them between a clinician and a retailer.

### What this changes for you

**You do not need to bring a prescription.** Bring one if you have it and they will use it — but its absence is not a problem, which is the opposite of the situation in most countries.

**You do not need to know your numbers.** Not your prescription, not your PD. They measure both.

**You can get glasses on a day you did not plan to.** This is the practical upshot and it is why visitors end up buying a spare pair. There is no appointment standing between you and the idea.

**And if something is wrong with your eyes rather than your vision** — pain, sudden change, floaters, an injury — **that is 안과 *(an-gwa)*, an eye clinic, and a different building.** An optician measures; a doctor diagnoses. Korea makes the first one easy, and the second one is [also walk-in](/en/now/seeing-a-doctor-in-korea-cost-walk-in/).

## What it actually costs

Korean opticians price the **frame** and the **lenses** separately, then add them. That is worth knowing before you walk in, because it is where the whole range comes from.

### Frames

| | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Plastic — **TR-90, Ultem** | **₩30,000 – 80,000** |
| Metal | ₩50,000 – 120,000 |
| **Titanium** | ₩100,000 – 250,000 |
| Rimless / semi-rimless | ₩100,000 – 300,000 |
| Imported designer | ₩200,000 upward |

### Lenses, per pair

The number is the **refractive index** — the higher it is, the thinner and lighter the lens for the same prescription.

| Index | Typical range | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **1.56** standard | **₩30,000 – 60,000** | Mild prescriptions |
| **1.60** | **₩40,000 – 80,000** | The common default |
| **1.67** high-index | **₩70,000 – 120,000** | Where the weight difference starts to show |
| **1.74** ultra high-index | ₩120,000 – 250,000 | Strong prescriptions |
| Progressive / varifocal | ₩250,000 – 450,000 | Add to the above |

### What Koreans actually buy

This is the row that matters, and it is the one you will not find on a tourist page.

| | Frame + lens | Total | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student / budget | Plastic + 1.60 | **₩80,000 – 120,000** | ~10 min |
| **The ordinary choice** | **Plastic or metal + 1.67** | **₩150,000 – 250,000** | **~10 min** |
| Going light | Titanium or rimless + 1.67 | ₩250,000 – 400,000 | same day |
| Strong prescription | Rimless + 1.74 | ₩350,000 – 600,000 | same day – 1 day |
| Progressives | Mid frame + varifocal | ₩500,000 – 700,000 | 1–3 days |

**Around ₩100,000 buys a real pair of glasses here.** Not a novelty pair — a proper frame with proper lenses, which is the number most Koreans quote when asked what cheap glasses cost.

## How that compares

| | Complete pair, out of pocket | Appointment needed? |
|---|---|---|
| **Korea** | **₩100,000 – 350,000** *(≈ US$72 – 250)* | **No — walk in** |
| United States | US$200 – 300 typical; **about $350 average without vision insurance** | Yes, eye exam usually separate |
| United Kingdom | £100 – 200 typical *(≈ US$125 – 250)* | Yes, sight test booked |
| Australia | A$100 – 600; **average about A$243** *(≈ US$160)* | Yes |

Two things to read off that table.

**On price, Korea is cheaper but not dramatically so at the mid-range.** Where it wins clearly is the **entry point** — around ₩100,000 for a complete, properly fitted pair — and the fact that **the eye test is included rather than billed separately**, which in the US in particular is a real extra cost.

**On time, it is not close.** Elsewhere you book a sight test, come back, choose, and wait for the lenses. In Korea the entire transaction is one visit of about ten minutes for a common prescription. **That is the actual reason to do it here.**

## Why Korean glasses feel lighter

Visitors say this a lot, and there is a concrete reason rather than a mystical one. It is two decisions the shop makes by default.

**The frames.** Plastic frames here are mostly **TR-90** and **Ultem** — engineering plastics that are noticeably lighter and springier than the acetate common elsewhere — and **titanium** and **rimless** designs are mainstream rather than premium-only. A rimless titanium frame weighs a fraction of a thick acetate one.

**The lenses.** In Korea **1.67 high-index is offered as the normal choice** rather than as an upsell, for anything past a mild prescription. Higher index means a thinner lens, and a thinner lens means less glass over your ears all day. **If you have a strong prescription, this is where nearly all of the weight difference lives** — the frame is only part of it.

So the honest version of "Korean glasses are light" is: **the light options are the default here, and elsewhere they are the upgrade.**

## Practical notes

**Bring your current prescription if you have one** — SPH, CYL, AXIS and ADD are international notation and a Korean optician reads it straight off. **But you do not need it.** They will test you either way, as above.

**They will measure your PD** (pupillary distance) themselves. You do not need to know it.

**Say what you want in plain terms.** *가볍게 해주세요 (ga-byeop-ge hae-ju-se-yo)* — "make it light." *얇은 렌즈로 해주세요 (yal-beun ren-jeu-ro hae-ju-se-yo)* — "thin lenses, please."

**Prescription sunglasses cost the same logic**: frame plus tinted lens. This is a common thing to have made here.

**A spare pair is the real move.** At Korean prices, having a second pair made is a decision most visitors would not make at home.

**Where:** any neighbourhood shopping street has several. **Namdaemun Market in Seoul has an entire building of optical wholesalers** — a concentration of eyewear traders in one place that few countries have.

## What this article does not claim

**The Korean prices are market observation, not an official survey.** The Korea Consumer Agency's price survey does not cover eyewear. Our ranges come from Korean consumer price discussions and published optician price lists, cross-checked against each other. Treat every figure here as a **range you should expect**, not a quotation, and expect variation between a Gangnam shop and a suburban one.

**The international figures are national market averages, not like-for-like products.** "A complete pair" means different lens grades and frame tiers in each market, and none of these tables is comparing an identical item. They are for orientation.

**Currency conversions are approximate** and move.

**"Ten minutes" applies to a common single-vision prescription.** Strong prescriptions, progressives, unusual lens coatings and specialist tints all take longer — sometimes days — because the lenses are ordered rather than held in stock.

**We have not assessed the quality of the eye tests.** Korean opticians (안경사) hold a national licence that covers refraction and dispensing, which is the basis for the shop-floor testing described above. **They are not ophthalmologists**, and a refraction is not an eye health examination. If you have an eye health concern rather than a vision-correction need — pain, sudden change, floaters, injury — that is 안과 *(an-gwa)*, an eye clinic, and a different building.

**We do not recommend specific shops**, here or anywhere on this site. We cannot verify individual businesses and they change.

**The lightness explanation is our reading of what is commonly stocked**, based on the materials and lens indices Korean opticians offer as standard. We have not weighed frames or surveyed shops, and we are not claiming Korean eyewear is objectively the lightest in the world.
