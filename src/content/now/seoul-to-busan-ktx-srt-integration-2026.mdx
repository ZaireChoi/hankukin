Korea has been running two separate high-speed rail companies on two separate ticketing systems. On **1 September 2026** that ends, and three things change for anyone travelling Seoul to Busan.

The fare goes down. The trains change name. And the app you booked with last time may no longer be the app you can book with.

There is also a fourth thing, and it is the one that will actually ruin a trip: **trains running 23–27 September 2026 are not on general sale.** More on that below.

## The fare, exactly

Korail's press release of 5 August 2026 gives two worked examples. These are the only two route fares stated in the release, and we are not extrapolating others.

| Route | Until 31 Aug 2026 | From 1 Sep 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seoul → Busan** | ₩59,800 | **₩54,400** | −₩5,400 |
| Yongsan → Gwangju Songjeong | ₩46,800 | ₩42,000 | −₩4,800 |

Fares checked **15 August 2026** against Korail's own announcement. The **10% average** applies to **KTX fares**: they are being brought down to the level SRT already charged, rather than SRT fares being brought up. Fares on the former SRT services from Suseo are the reference level, so do not expect those to fall.

The lower fares attach to trains **running from 1 September**, and those trains went on sale on **5 August at 10:00** — so a ticket bought in August for a September journey is already a September-priced ticket. Korail states the sale date and the service date; it does not spell out a booking-date rule beyond that.

## What "integration" actually means, and what it does not

**It does mean:** the two fleets are operated as one. SRT trains are **renamed KTX-Sancheon** (KTX-산천). One app, one membership, one set of rules.

**It does not mean the trains all leave from the same place.** This is the single most common misunderstanding, and it will put you at the wrong station.

Korea's high-speed services to Busan run on two different lines from two different Seoul-area stations:

| Departure station | High-speed line | Where it is, and what serves it |
|---|---|---|
| **서울역 Seoul Station** | Gyeongbu high-speed line | Central Seoul. Subway Lines 1 and 4, the Airport Railroad, GTX-A (Gyeongui–Jungang is a separate adjacent station of the same name) |
| **수서역 Suseo Station** | Suseo high-speed line | South-east Seoul. Subway Line 3, the Suin–Bundang Line, GTX-A |

Station connections per Seoul Metro's own station index, checked 15 August 2026.

Getting between the two is a journey across the city, not a walk — but note the one line that appears in both rows. **GTX-A serves Seoul Station and Suseo**, which makes it the connection to look at first if your ticket turns out to leave from the other station. Budget for the fare either way: [the subway fare and how transfers are charged are here](/en/now/seoul-transport-fares-subway-bus-taxi/). If you are coming straight from the airport, the Airport Railroad runs to Seoul Station, not to Suseo ([airport routes and costs](/en/now/incheon-airport-to-seoul-train-bus-taxi/)).

The former SRT route from Suseo, as published by SR, calls at: 수서 – 동탄 – 평택지제 – 천안아산 – 오송 – 대전 – 김천(구미) – 서대구 – 동대구 – 신경주 – 울산(통도사) – 부산.

A ticket that says **수서** is not a ticket from Seoul Station. Merging the companies did not move the tracks. Read the departure station on your booking before you get in a taxi.

## The app is now a gate, not a convenience

Since 3 August 2026 the combined app is **코레일+ (Korail+)**, delivered as an update to 코레일톡 (Korail Talk) or as a new install. To use it — and to get the reduced fares and the expanded benefits — you need to be on the **integrated railway membership**.

| If you had… | What you must do |
|---|---|
| A Korail account only | Nothing. Converted automatically |
| An SR (SRT) account only | **Convert manually** on the separate web page linked from the app or site |
| Both accounts | **Convert manually** |

During August the old split still applies: KTX trains are booked in Korail+ or the old Korail Talk, SRT trains in the SRT app. From 1 September, integrated trains are booked in **Korail+ or www.korail.com**.

If you have used SRT before on a previous trip to Korea, assume your account needs converting and do it before you need a ticket, not while you are standing in a station.

## The Chuseok trap

**Trains running 23–27 September 2026 are not sold through normal booking.** They are released through a separate holiday sale with its own calendar, its own dedicated web page, and a hard completion limit.

This is the Chuseok travel period. If you arrive in Korea in mid-September expecting to book a Seoul–Busan train for the 25th the way you would book any other day, you are working from the wrong calendar: those seats are distributed through the holiday sale below, on the dates below, and the sale is finished before the holiday starts.

**Priority sale** — 3–4 September, 09:00–15:00. For over-65s, registered disabled travellers, designated national-merit recipients, and (new this year) pregnant travellers. Gyeongbu-line tickets, which includes Seoul–Busan, are sold on **3 September**.

**General sale** — 7–11 September, 07:00–13:00, split by line and train type:

| Date | What is on sale |
|---|---|
| 7 Sep | All conventional trains (ITX-Saemaeul, ITX-Maeum, Mugunghwa) |
| 8 Sep | KTX on the Gyeongjeon, Gangneung, Donghae, Jungang, Jungbunaeryuk lines |
| 9 Sep | KTX on the Honam and Jeolla lines |
| 10 Sep | KTX departing from or arriving at **Suseo** on the Gyeongbu, Gyeongjeon, Donghae, Honam and Jeolla lines |
| 11 Sep | **KTX on the Gyeongbu line** — this is Seoul → Busan |

Two details that catch people out:

- **You have three minutes.** Once you enter the general-sale page you must complete the booking within three minutes. (Priority sale allows five minutes; severely disabled members get thirty.)
- **Reserving is not buying.** Payment opens at 00:00 on 12 September. General-sale reservations must be paid **by 15 September**, priority-sale reservations **by 18 September**. Unpaid reservations are cancelled automatically and passed to the waiting list.

Only integrated members can book the Chuseok sale at all. Korail states this explicitly.

## More seats, and where they went

From 1 September the daily high-speed timetable expands.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Weekday services/day | 379 (Seoul 257, Suseo 122) | **402** (Seoul 270, Suseo 132) |
| Weekend services/day | 431 (Seoul 299, Suseo 132) | **457** (Seoul 313, Suseo 144) |

That is **+15,679 seats on a weekday** and **+17,655 on a weekend day** — about 116,000 a week. Korail is also putting its highest-capacity trainsets (955 seats per set) on Suseo departures, and adding new Suseo services routed via Seodaejeon and Gupo.

The practical read: **Suseo has been the harder station to get a seat from, and most of the new capacity went there.** If Seoul Station is full for your date, check Suseo before you give up — and check where Suseo actually is relative to where you are staying.

## Discounts that now cover every high-speed train

Previously several Korail discounts did not apply to SRT services. From 1 September they apply across the whole high-speed network.

| Who | Discount |
|---|---|
| Pregnant travellers | 40% off, or first-class supplement waived |
| Age 13–24 | 10–30% off, depending on how full the train is |
| Age 25–33 | 10–40% off, depending on how full the train is |
| Anyone, online special | 10–30% off on lightly booked KTX services |
| Repeat travellers (N-card) | 15–40% off KTX, 15–30% off conventional trains, on a named route |

The **N카드 (N-card)** is worth a look if you are doing the same route more than once: you nominate the route, period and number of trips, and it deducts from a count. It is only available in Korail+.

Two more changes on the former SRT routes: **free-seating carriages** are being introduced for commuters holding season or free-seating tickets, and **standing tickets** are being expanded — which is the difference between travelling and not travelling on a day when Suseo is full.

Mileage is now earned on Suseo departures too, at **5% of what you actually paid** — usable on tickets, on penalty payments, at station shops and cafés, and to top up a Rail Plus transport card.

There is also a transfer discount worth knowing if you are going somewhere small: buy a KTX ticket to or from Suseo **together with** a connecting conventional train, and the conventional leg is **30% off**.

## Rules that got better, quietly

These are the parts nobody announces loudly and everybody needs eventually.

- **Changing a ticket.** Old SRT tickets could only be changed on the day of travel, up to three hours before departure. From 1 September any high-speed ticket can be changed **within seven days either side of the travel date, up to 30 minutes before departure, with no fee.**
- **Cancelling immediately.** The Korail rule waiving the cancellation penalty if you cancel **within 10 minutes of paying** (up to 30 minutes before departure, twice a day) now covers all integrated members.
- **Delays.** The compensation scale was refined this month. Previously any delay over 60 minutes paid a flat 50% of the fare.

| Delay | Compensation |
|---|---|
| 60–90 min | 50% of fare |
| 90–120 min | 75% of fare |
| Over 120 min | 100% of fare |

## Will the fare go back up in three years?

You will see this claimed. Korail issued a specific rebuttal on 7 August 2026: **nothing has been decided about restoring fares or reducing mileage after three years.**

The three-year figure is a **minimum obligation period**, set during the Fair Trade Commission's merger review to secure the public benefit — a floor under the benefits, not an expiry date on them. The Commission and the Ministry signed an agreement to jointly monitor Korail's fares, seat supply and service against that commitment for those three years. Changing a rail fare in Korea requires consultation between agencies, a filing by Korail and acceptance by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport under the Railroad Business Act. It is not something the operator can simply do.

What the Ministry's integration plan does say is that after the three years, fares will be reviewed against demand, service levels, financial impact and public burden. Reviewed — not automatically raised.

## What this article does not claim

**We do not give journey times or timetables.** Both operators' booking systems block automated queries, and we will not publish a journey time we could not read off an operator's own page. Check departure and arrival times in Korail+ or at www.korail.com when you book.

**We do not give the Suseo–Busan fare.** Korail's release states only two worked examples — Seoul–Busan and Yongsan–Gwangju Songjeong. Suseo is a different distance from Seoul Station and we did not find its post-integration fare in a primary source.

**The two fares above are as Korail published them.** The release does not specify seat class or passenger type for those figures; first-class (특실) fares are higher, and the discounts listed above change what you actually pay. Treat ₩54,400 as the standard adult reference fare, not as the number on every ticket.

**We do not give express bus fares.** We intended to compare the coach from Seoul Express Bus Terminal. The operator's booking site did not respond when we checked on 15 August 2026, so there is no bus row in the tables above rather than a guessed one.
