You sit down, you order, the bill comes, and you have no way of knowing whether you were charged a normal price or a tourist one. Most guides answer this with ranges so wide they are useless — "expect ₩8,000–20,000 for a meal" tells you nothing.

Korea happens to have a better answer, and almost nobody outside the country uses it.

The **Korea Consumer Agency** runs a price portal called **참가격 (Chamgagyeok)**. Every month it publishes the average price of the *same eight restaurant dishes* in every province, alongside the price of a haircut, a bathhouse entry and a night in a budget inn. It is a government price survey, not a review site, and the underlying data comes from the national statistics office.

Everything below is the **June 2026** release, read from the portal on 14 August 2026.

## Seoul is dearest — in half the cases

The chart makes the useful point faster than a paragraph can. **Seoul tops the table in four dishes out of eight**, and the gaps are not small: naengmyeon costs **30% more** in Seoul than in South Jeolla, gimbap **33% more**.

But look at the other four, because this is where the received wisdom breaks.

**Bibimbap is more expensive in Jeonbuk (₩12,100) than in Seoul (₩11,769).** Jeonbuk contains Jeonju, and Jeonju bibimbap is the version the dish is named after. The home of the dish charges the most for it. That is not a rip-off; it is what happens when a regional speciality becomes a destination in itself.

**A kimchi jjigae set costs ₩10,800 in Daejeon and ₩8,654 in Seoul** — 25% more in a city most visitors have never considered. Averages of small samples move easily, and this is the kind of number to hold loosely, but it is a useful corrective to "everything is cheaper outside Seoul."

**Jajangmyeon barely moves at all.** ₩6,769 to ₩7,750 nationwide — a 14% spread, the tightest of the eight. Korean-Chinese restaurants price this dish almost identically everywhere, which makes it the closest thing Korea has to a fixed-price meal.

## Reading the table as a budget

Two anchors make these numbers usable.

**The cheapest full sit-down meal in the survey is the kimchi jjigae set** — ₩8,278 in South Jeolla, ₩8,654 in Seoul. It comes with rice and side dishes. If you want one number for "a proper Korean lunch," this is it.

**A roll of gimbap costs about two and a half subway rides.** Seoul's base subway fare is ₩1,550 with a transit card; gimbap is ₩3,838. That ratio is worth carrying because it tells you when something is expensive *in Korean terms* rather than in your home currency. (Fares are in [what Seoul transport actually costs](/en/now/seoul-transport-fares-subway-bus-taxi/).)

And the ceiling: **grilled pork belly is the expensive meal**, ₩21,321 for 200g in Seoul. Samgyeopsal is a social dish ordered by the portion for a table, so a group dinner escalates quickly. It is the one item where the price will surprise you if you have been eating ₩9,000 lunches.

## The rest of a Korean day

The same portal surveys personal services. June 2026, Seoul, with the national range beside it:

| | Seoul | Range across regions |
|---|---|---|
| **Public bathhouse**, one entry | 11,000 | 8,308 – 11,000 |
| **Men's haircut** | 13,231 | 12,286 – 16,444 |
| **Women's haircut** | 24,462 | 15,300 – 25,500 |
| **Dry cleaning**, a suit | 11,538 | 8,800 – 13,500 |
| **Budget inn** (여관), one night | 53,769 | 39,167 – 53,769 |

Three things worth noticing.

**The bathhouse is ₩11,000 in Seoul** — and Seoul is the most expensive in the country for it. For a first-time visitor this is the single cheapest way to spend two hours doing something unmistakably Korean, and the price is barely more than a bowl of noodles.

**Men's haircuts are dearest in Gangwon (₩16,444), not Seoul.** Women's haircuts are dearest in Incheon (₩25,500), just ahead of Seoul. Neither follows the pattern you would guess.

**The budget-inn figure is not a hotel price.** A 여관 is an older, plain guesthouse — private room with its own bathroom, no service. It is the floor of Korean accommodation, and at ₩53,769 in Seoul it sets the number below which a listing should make you suspicious.

## What this article does not claim

**These are averages, not prices you are entitled to.** The Consumer Agency surveys a sample of restaurants per region. A famous naengmyeon house in Seoul will charge well above ₩12,615, and a neighbourhood place will charge below it. The average tells you where the middle is, not what any one shop will ask.

**Small samples move.** Some regions are surveyed at fewer establishments than others, and a single closure or new entrant can shift a provincial average by several hundred won month to month. Treat the *shape* — Seoul dearest, Jeolla cheapest, jajangmyeon flat — as solid, and any single figure as approximate.

**The Consumer Agency itself warns against comparing across time periods**, because the surveyed establishments and product quality are not held constant between months. We have not built any trend claims here for that reason.

**Street food is not in this survey at all.** Every price above is a sit-down restaurant. Market stalls and street vendors run lower, and what they contain is a separate question — [some of it is not what it looks like](/en/now/korean-street-food-hidden-pork-and-fish/) if you avoid pork or fish.

**We have not converted anything to dollars or euros**, deliberately. Exchange rates move faster than this page does, and a converted figure that is six months stale is worse than no figure. Convert at today's rate yourself.

**Re-check before you rely on it.** This is the June 2026 release, read on 14 August 2026. The portal updates monthly and the URL is in the sources above.
