In the sixth episode of *Goblin*, Ji Eun-tak blows out a birthday candle on a breakwater and the
Goblin appears behind her, buckwheat flowers in hand. It is the scene the drama is remembered by,
and it is one of the few from the series that you can stand inside almost exactly as it was shot.

The breakwater is real, working infrastructure — not a set. It sits on the stretch of coast between
Jumunjin and Yeongjin beaches in Gangneung, and Gangneung City lists it among the filming locations
on its official tourism portal. There is no gate, no ticket and no opening time. You walk out onto
the concrete and that is the whole of it.

What surprises most visitors is the scale. On screen the breakwater reads as a narrow finger of
stone against an empty sea. In person it is wider, lower, and the working harbour sits close behind
you — which is why the framing below matters more than the spot itself. Turn the wrong way and you
photograph a fishing port.

Getting there costs more than a city ride — see [what Korean transport actually costs](/en/now/seoul-transport-fares-subway-bus-taxi/) before you budget the day.

Come outside July and August if you can. The scene's mood belongs to cold, flat light, and the
summer crowds queue at the tip for the same three metres of concrete.
