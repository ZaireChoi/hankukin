The English-language advice about attending a Korean music show is remarkably consistent. Apply through the official site. Bring your passport. Get there early. All of it is true.

None of the guides we read mentions the condition that will actually stop you, which is on KBS's own application page in plain Korean: **Music Bank does not book one seat. It books two, and both people have to walk in.**

## Two names, given at the start, and no swapping them

The application takes a companion's name at the moment you apply. Not later, not at the door.

The page then closes the obvious workaround. Turning up alone with a friend's name on the booking does not work: it says both people must arrive at the stated time and be identified before either can go in. And identification means the physical document — the page rejects photocopies and photographs of an ID.

So a solo traveller who wins the lottery has not won a seat. They have won two seats they cannot use.

This is worth knowing before you plan the trip rather than after, and it is the sort of thing that never appears in a guide written by someone who went with a friend.

## The rest of the KBS conditions, in the order they bite

All of these are on the same page, read on 18 August 2026.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Applications open** | Thursday 09:00, one week before the broadcast |
| **Applications close** | Friday 24:00 — the same week |
| **Results** | On the application page from Monday 18:00; by text message from Thursday 14:00 |
| **Seats** | Assigned at random, with the seat number given in the result |
| **Age** | Under 15 cannot enter, ticket or no ticket |
| **On the day** | Ticket exchange from 14:00; entry 16:10–16:40 |
| **Once the live show starts** | No entry |

Two of those rows matter more to a visitor than to anyone else.

**The results go out by text message.** There is a second route — the page and a mobile ticket in KBS's own app — so a Korean number is not strictly required to find out. But the notification most people rely on is an SMS, and if you are reading this while planning from abroad, that is one more thing on the pile of [Korean services that assume a Korean phone number](/en/decode/korea-esim-no-phone-number/).

**Entry closes at 16:40 for a show that begins later.** Music Bank moved to 17:15 on Fridays from 10 July 2026, per the programme's own notice board. The gap is not slack. It is the doors being shut before broadcast.

There are two more conditions that guides tend to leave out because they are not obstacles, exactly. Filming or recording inside the studio is forbidden, and the page says being caught means deletion of the material and removal from the building. And attending is itself consent: the audience is filmed, and the page states that by taking part you agree to that footage being used on air and online. If you would rather not be on Korean television, this is the paragraph to read twice.

## SBS published its instructions as a picture

Inkigayo has an audience page too. We opened it expecting a similar list.

The page came back with no text at all — a heading, and then nothing. Our first reading was that SBS had left the page empty.

Then we took a screenshot, and the instructions were right there: a tall pink graphic explaining the whole process. **The page is one image.** There is not a single line of selectable text in the instructions.

<ChartPageReadable caption="Both pages read on 18 August 2026. The row we misread first is the second one: a page whose instructions are an image returns nothing to a text reader, which looks identical to a page with nothing on it. The 'newest date' row is what each page shows about its own age — the KBS worked example gives days and months without a year, while the phone mockups inside the SBS image show episodes 1244 to 1247, with entry dates in November 2024." />

That distinction is not pedantry. A page made of text can be translated by the browser, indexed by a search engine, read aloud by a screen reader, and copied into a translation app. A page made of one image can do none of those things without a separate tool that reads pictures. For a foreign fan, SBS's instructions are not in Korean. They are in nothing.

We have published [an article arguing that Korean sites' English pages are older copies of the Korean ones](/en/now/english-pages-korea-out-of-date/). This is a step below that. There is no English page here to be out of date, because there is no text to translate in the first place.

## What the picture says

We read it with our eyes, off screenshots, so that this article does not simply report that a page was unreadable and stop there.

The route is the broadcaster's app rather than a web page: **SBS play → My → viewing and application history → the audience application banner**, then choose a date. Results arrive as an app push notification, and entry is by a ticket held inside the app.

That is a coherent system, and for someone already inside it, probably a smoother one than KBS's. It is also a system that begins with installing a Korean broadcaster's app and creating an account in it — and we could not test what that account creation asks of someone with a foreign passport and no Korean address. We say more about that below.

One detail from inside the image is worth recording. The phone mockups used to illustrate the steps show Inkigayo episodes 1244 to 1247, with entry dates through November 2024. The programme has since passed episode 1320. The instruction graphic has not been redrawn since it was made.

## The free ticket that people sell

The strongest thing on either broadcaster's site is not on the application page. It is on Music Bank's notice board, and KBS wrote it twice — once in Korean, once in English.

The English in that block is not our translation. It is the sentence KBS chose to put in English, which tells you who the notice is aimed at.

The notice goes further than a warning. It says selling these tickets for profit is illegal, that tickets identified as traded will be refused entry, and that the financial loss falls on the people who did the trading. The application page carries the same rule from the other direction: duplicate bookings and any sale or transfer of a ticket mean the booking is void and entry is refused.

And KBS did something we have not seen a Korean broadcaster do elsewhere. Alongside the notice it published the two applicants caught trading on one recording day, with their names and IDs partly masked.

The application page describes no price and no payment step at any point. Read together with a broadcaster calling the sale of these tickets a crime, the shape of the thing is clear enough: money changing hands for a Music Bank seat is the warning sign, not the transaction.

## What we could not check, and will not guess

**Whether either application can be completed on a foreign passport.** Both routes need an account with the broadcaster, and we did not create one. Secondary sources describe a passport-based registration path at KBS and an alien-registration-card requirement at SBS. We could not confirm either at source — the SBS conditions are inside the image, and the account screens are behind a login. Until we have seen them ourselves, we are not repeating either claim as fact.

**Whether the paid "music show" packages sold by tour agencies are what KBS is warning about.** Several English guides recommend buying an Inkigayo package from a travel company. Those companies may have an arrangement we know nothing about, or they may not. We are not calling them illegal and we are not calling them safe. What we can say is the narrow thing: KBS's own notice says selling Music Bank tickets for profit is illegal and that traded tickets will be refused at the door. If you are considering paying anyone for a seat at either show, that sentence is the one to put to the seller before you pay.

**Whether SBS's instruction image has changed since we read it.** An image cannot be diffed the way text can. We recorded what it showed on 18 August 2026, and a reader arriving later should open it and look.

## What this article does not claim

We describe two programmes: KBS's Music Bank and SBS's Inkigayo. We make no claim about MBC, Mnet, MBC M or any other music show, and the rules at those are not the rules here.

Everything above was read on 18 August 2026 and applies to that day. Application windows, entry times and age limits are the kind of thing a broadcaster changes without announcing it widely, so treat the dates as a shape rather than a promise and open the pages before you rely on them.

We did not attend either recording, we did not apply, and we hold no account with either broadcaster. This article reports what the two pages said, not what happens at the door.
