Korea is hard to be alone in, and the rules say so out loud.

We spent a day reading what two broadcasters require of anyone who wants to sit in a music show audience. KBS books Music Bank [two seats at a time, with the companion's name entered at the application and both people required at the door](/en/scenes/music-show-audience-korea-what-the-rules-say/). A solo traveller who wins that lottery has not won a seat. Restaurants portion for two. Booking forms open with how many.

Itaewon is the exception. Not because the people are friendlier — that is not a thing we can measure — but because of what the neighbourhood is made of.

## The night I gave up and he did not

In 2011 I took a friend to Itaewon. He wanted to keep going; I did not. I left him there and went back to the hotel alone, which is a polite way of saying I abandoned him on a street corner near midnight.

He reappeared three days later. He had made a great many friends, he said, Korean and foreign, and he seemed faintly puzzled that I had left.

I am telling you this with the date attached because **2011 was fifteen years ago** and this article is not a promise. The army has largely gone. There has been a disaster. The rents have changed. What has not changed is the thing that made three days possible, and that is worth explaining before anything else.

## Why this one neighbourhood is different

Itaewon sits beside what was, for decades, the United States Army garrison at Yongsan, and among a cluster of embassies. Both facts produced the same kind of resident: **people who were here for a while and then would not be.**

A neighbourhood built for people passing through develops habits. Someone arriving alone is not an event. Nobody is waiting for the rest of your party.

One correction, because it gets repeated carelessly and we nearly did it too. **The base did not simply leave.** The Eighth Army headquarters moved to Pyeongtaek in 2017, and much of the land has been returned in stages — but parts of the Yongsan site remain in American use, including a hotel, a helipad and an access area, classified as retained land rather than returned. The honest verb is not *left*. It is *shrank*.

## What to eat: walk uphill

Come out of Itaewon Station exit 3 and walk straight, and you reach the bottom of **Usadan-ro**. Go up.

This is the halal quarter, and Yongsan District has treated it as one — it ran a project to develop Usadan-ro and Usadan-ro 10-gil as a halal food street, and in 2017 surveyed the restaurants and printed a map of them in Korean-English and English-Arabic editions. Roughly fifty halal restaurants and grocers sit in this small area.

At the top of the hill is the reason all of it is there.

The **Seoul Central Mosque** opened on 21 May 1976, the first mosque in Korea, on land the government granted in 1969 and with construction funded by Saudi Arabia and other Muslim countries. Its own page describes what surrounds it plainly: halal restaurants, a halal butcher, an Islamic bookshop, a halal supermarket, a travel agency.

For a visitor this is the practical shape of it: **the restaurants and the mosque are the same walk.** Eat on the way up or the way down.

If you are working out whether you can eat anything in the rest of the country, we have [a separate article on how Korean food handles halal and vegetarian requirements](/en/now/is-korean-food-halal-vegetarian/), and the short answer is that it is harder than it looks.

**They will lend you clothes.** If you arrive dressed too briefly, the page says there is a changing room beside the guard post stocked with skirts and headscarves, and that you put them on and then look around. You are not sent away.

**You cannot go inside the prayer hall.** It is the first line on the list of things strictly restricted, along with pets, photographing during prayers, photographing anyone without their agreement, smoking, drinking and shouting.

**Hours are 10:30 to 17:00,** and the page names the times to avoid: the half hour either side of each prayer, and **Friday between 12:00 and 15:00**, when it is too busy for anyone to show you around.

## What to buy: the street the army left behind

Walk out of the station on the other side, past the Hamilton Hotel towards Bogwang-ro, and you are on the **antique furniture street**.

Its origin is the whole argument of this article compressed into one fact. In the 1960s American soldiers rotating home sold the furniture they had been living with. Dealers grew up around that trade, then began importing, and now the run of shops carries European, American and Asian pieces — furniture, but also clocks, teacups and small ornaments. The stretch runs roughly from Itaewon Station past the Cheonghwa Apartments towards the Saudi Arabian embassy.

We are not going to give you a shop count. The association's own website would not load for us on 20 August 2026, at either address we tried, so any number we printed would be borrowed from somewhere we had not checked.

The other shopping is more ordinary and easier: the covered clothing market near the station, small stalls of shirts, coats and bags. It is not glamorous. It is cheap, it is indoors, and it is open when the weather is not.

## When it feels like a festival — the honest answer

Two festivals. **One of them has stopped.**

The **Itaewon Global Village Festival** ran every October from 2002. Seoul's own culture portal describes it as the Korean festival with the largest embassy participation — around forty countries sending parade teams, performers and crafts — with a global street parade, a world culture awards event, and, in the portal's own words, a late-night DJ party as the highlight. More than a million visitors a year.

The same page lists its dates as **15–16 October 2022**.

That was the last edition. The Itaewon crowd crush happened two weeks later, on 29 October 2022, and 159 people died in an alley off the main street. The festival has not run since. In September 2025 the district office hosted a public discussion on whether and how to restart it. As of this writing there is no announced date.

**So do not travel in early October expecting that festival.** Check before you plan around it.

What is running is the **antique and vintage festival** on the furniture street, held twice a year in spring and autumn — flea market stalls, antique auctions, buskers, caricaturists, wine tasting. A spring 2026 edition took place. We could not open the organisers' site to confirm exact dates, so we are telling you the season and not the day.

And Halloween: the crowds came back in 2025, under heavy management. Police ran a special operation across the Halloween period with roughly 4,900 officers citywide; several hundred were deployed in Itaewon itself, directing one-way pedestrian flow with barriers. The alley where the crush happened was closed to entry.

## The practical part, kept short

**Assume the station may stop stopping.** Seoul Metro's guidance for the Halloween period is that Itaewon, Hongdae and Seongsu stations may be run through without stopping *depending on conditions* — it is not a published timetable, it is a decision taken when the platforms fill. On 31 October 2025 that decision came at about 11pm, announced by emergency text.

The practical consequence is not a time to memorise. It is this: **on a crowded night, plan your way out on the assumption that the station is not available.** Walk to the next station, or arrange a pickup point away from the main street, before you need it.

Most nights of the year, none of this applies and the station is a station.

## Going alone

Here is what we can and cannot tell you.

We cannot tell you that you will make friends. That is not a fact and we have no way to check it. What we can tell you is the structure: this is a neighbourhood of people who arrived from somewhere else and knew they were temporary, where a mosque lends clothes to strangers who turn up underdressed, where the shops were built out of what departing soldiers left behind. Arriving alone is the normal case here, not the exception.

In Korea, turning twenty and going to Itaewon is close to a rite of passage — the first place many young Koreans go once they are legally adults. You will be walking the same streets on the same night, for entirely different reasons.

My friend stayed three days. I went back to the hotel. Fifteen years later I am still the one who has to explain himself.
