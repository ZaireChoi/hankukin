Korea's national tourism body describes this island, on its English site, as "a destination well known to fans of the drama ."

The sentence ends there. The drama has no name in it.

It is not censorship and it is not laziness. Korean writing puts titles inside angle brackets — 〈겨울연가〉 — and somewhere in the pipeline those brackets became the ASCII kind. `<Winter Sonata>` is not a title to a web browser. It is an unknown HTML tag, and the browser swallowed it whole. The words are still in the page source. They are simply not on the page.

So the one word that tells a K-drama viewer why this island exists as a destination — <strong>Winter Sonata</strong> — is invisible on the official English page about it, while the Korean side of the same organisation reads normally. We have written before about [English pages in Korea drifting from the Korean ones](/en/now/english-pages-korea-out-of-date/); this is a smaller and stranger version of it, a fact deleted by a punctuation mark.

The rest of the island is more tractable. It is also, in a few specific places, on a clock.

## The island is in one province and the boat is in another

Nami Island is registered at 1 Namiseom-gil, Namsan-myeon, <strong>Chuncheon</strong>, Gangwon. You cannot get there from that address. The wharf is at 1024 Bukhangangbyeon-ro, Gapyeong-eup, <strong>Gapyeong</strong>, Gyeonggi — a different city in a different province, on the other side of the water.

The operator says so itself, at the top of its own directions page, before anything else:

That warning is on the page because people get it wrong, and the tourism board's own destination entry is a way to get it wrong: it lists the Chuncheon address under the heading "Address", with no wharf. Typed into a navigation app it will route you to the far bank. The operator's English footer is the model here — it prints both, labelled, with postcodes: wharf 12422, island 24464. If you are handing an address to a taxi driver, hand over the Gapyeong one.

For navigation, the operator gives the name search as 남이섬 선착장 *(Nam-i-seom seon-chak-jang)*, Nami Island wharf.

## ₩19,000, and one row of the fee table with no price in it

Admission is called a visa fee, because the island bills itself as a micronation. It includes the round-trip boat.

| Ticket | Ferry both ways | Motorboat in, ferry out |
|---|---|---|
| Regular | ₩19,000 | ₩29,000 |
| Discount | ₩16,000 | ₩26,000 |
| Special | ₩13,000 | ₩23,000 |

Discount is middle and high school age, and 70 and over. Special is 36 months to elementary age. Groups of 20 or more take 10 per cent off the total, and the group and free-entry benefits <strong>apply only if you go both ways by ferry</strong> — take the motorboat and they disappear. The motorboat needs at least two passengers to leave and carries five.

Two things here are worth reading slowly.

The first is a difference between the two language versions. The Korean fee page lists <strong>four</strong> discount categories: students, holders of a severe-disability welfare card, national merit recipients and their bereaved families, and over-70s. The English page lists two — students and over-70s. Most foreign visitors will not qualify for the two that vanished, since both are documented with Korean state cards; but the English page has quietly become a shorter list rather than a translated one. The Korean page also ends its free-entry note with a line the English page does not carry at all: 증빙서류 지참 필수 *(jeung-bing-seo-ryu ji-cham pil-su)*, proof documents are required. If you are turning up expecting a free or reduced entry, bring the paper.

The second is stranger. The fee table has a fourth row, and the row has conditions but no amount. In both languages it reads: early bird, meaning a ticket bought and boarded before 08:00; from December to March, tickets bought after 18:01; from April to November, tickets bought after 18:31. Three discount conditions, no discount figure, in either language. <strong>We are not going to invent one.</strong> If you are counting on an evening saving, ask at the window.

And the early-bird condition contains a small trap of its own, which you only see if you read the fee page and the timetable together. Boarding before 08:00 is not possible on the scheduled service, because the first boat is 08:00 — except in one ten-week window, which is the next thing.

## Ten weeks when the island's own last-ferry line is wrong

Ferries run from 08:00 to 21:00 into the island, with the last boat off at 21:05. Every 30 minutes early, every 10 to 20 minutes from 09:00 to 18:00, every 30 minutes again after that.

Except that this year they do not, twice. The schedule page carries a notice of additional 2026 service:

That window opens ten days from the date on this article, and it is exactly the autumn foliage stretch — the same weeks our [foliage timing article](/en/now/korea-autumn-foliage-when-and-where/) is about.

Here is the problem. Directly underneath that notice, on the same page, in both Korean and English, sit the summary lines: first ferry 08:00, last ferry 21:00. They are not updated to match the notice above them. The summary lines are the ones people read, because they are formatted as the answer.

So for the ten weeks when this island is busiest, its own schedule page tells you two different things about the first and last boat, and the one that looks authoritative is the one that is wrong. The tourism board's page is worse: it gives the ferry as 08:00–09:00 and 09:00–18:00 and stops, omitting the 18:00–21:00 band entirely. Read that page and you would think the last boat is at six.

Our reading, which we are marking as ours: <strong>treat the notice as the schedule and the summary line as a leftover</strong>, and if you are planning around the last boat rather than the first, telephone. The number is +82 31 580 8114.

## The 16:30 problem

There is one direct coach between Seoul and the island, run for the operator by a contractor, and it is a round trip rather than two separate journeys. Outbound it leaves Hongik University Station Exit 4 at 09:00, Namsan Yejang bus transfer lot near Myeongdong at 09:40 and Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Exit 9 at 09:50, reaching the island at 11:30.

It comes back at <strong>16:30</strong>.

That is the whole trap, and it is not hidden — it is simply printed as a timetable rather than as a consequence. Booking the coach buys you five hours on an island whose boats run for another four and a half after you have left, five and a half in the autumn window. The late-entry discount row you read about above is unreachable on the coach. So is the 21:30 boat. Pets are not allowed on it either.

If your day is going to end late, the railway is the answer and not the coach.

## Getting back: the last three trains, and the one that stops short

Gapyeong Station is 1.5 km from the wharf: about 20 minutes on foot, or five minutes by taxi or the 10-4 local bus. The operator publishes those figures; we have not walked it.

From there, these are the last departures toward Seoul in Korail's own timetable files, in force from 1 September 2026:

| | Weekday | Weekend and holiday |
|---|---|---|
| Last ITX-Cheongchun to Yongsan | 22:32 | 22:30 |
| Last Gyeongchun Line to Cheongnyangni | 22:26 | 22:24 |
| Last Gyeongchun Line to Sangbong | 23:11 | 23:12 |
| Last train of any kind | 23:57 | 23:37 |

The last row is the one to look at twice. Those final services do not go to Seoul at all — they terminate at Pyeongnae-hopyeong, in Namyangju, which is on the same line and roughly halfway. A visitor watching the platform display and boarding the last train because it is the last train ends up in a suburb at midnight with a taxi fare instead of a subway ride.

So the real last train to Seoul from Gapyeong is <strong>23:11 on a weekday</strong>, or 23:12 at the weekend, and it goes to Sangbong. If the station you need is Cheongnyangni or Yongsan rather than Sangbong, your cut-off moves back to around half past ten.

Against a 21:05 last boat, that is a comfortable margin. Against the 21:30 autumn boat it is still comfortable. It stops being comfortable if you assume the coach and the train are interchangeable, or if you have been reading the 18:00 figure on the tourism board's page and have not planned an evening at all.

We have not printed a rail fare. Korail's fare tables on that page cover KTX and the long-distance ITX classes only; there is no ITX-Cheongchun or Gyeongchun Line fare table published there, and we are not going to estimate one. Our [Seoul transport fares article](/en/now/seoul-transport-fares-subway-bus-taxi/) covers how the transit-card distance fare is built, which is the mechanism the Gyeongchun Line stopping service runs on.

Driving instead: parking is ₩6,000 for a small car and ₩10,000 for a large one, covering the first 12 hours, then ₩1,000 an hour. Registering the vehicle on the Kakao T app before you enter brings it to ₩5,500 and ₩6,000. Card only — the car parks do not take cash.

The same notice goes on to suggest a raincoat and boots and lists what is open indoors — the picture-book playground, the UNICEF lounge and five galleries, all free once you are through the gate. Nothing about it is Korean-specific information. It is simply advice that exists on one side of the site and not the other, and the practical effect is that a visitor checking the English pages on a wet morning finds nothing and stays in Seoul. This is the reverse of our [Cheonggyecheon article](/en/guides/cheonggyecheon-seoul-entrances-rain-closure/), where rain genuinely does close the place; here it does not, and only the Korean page says so.

The entry rules, to be fair, are properly translated. The whole island is non-smoking outside designated points, on the strength of that UNICEF certification rather than as a preference. No camping, cooking, tents or shade shelters. No amplifiers. No drones. Dogs under 20 kg on a lead are welcome, five legally designated dangerous breeds are not, and assistance dogs carrying the official marker are admitted without restriction under the Act on Welfare of Persons with Disabilities. And the one that catches cyclists: <strong>your own bicycle, e-scooter or kickboard cannot be loaded onto the ferry.</strong> Rent theirs on the island or walk.

## What this article does not claim

Every figure here is the operator's or the railway's, read on 8 September 2026, and none of it has been tested against an actual boat or train. The train times are the departures in Korail's own timetable files for services from 1 September 2026, and timetables change. The 21:30 autumn ferry is stated by the operator as running on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays, but the operator does not say whether 21:30 is the last boat *into* the island or *off* it — the normal pair is 21:00 in and 21:05 out, and we have not assumed the same five minutes applies. We have not printed a rail fare or an early-bird discount amount, because neither is published anywhere we could open. And we have not been to this island: the photography section above tells you what the operator forbids, not where to stand.
