For fifty-nine years, you could not walk all the way around Deoksugung. The stone wall road —
돌담길 *(dol-dam-gil)* — ran along the palace wall until it hit the British Embassy compound, which
had closed the northern stretch since 1959. Seoul started knocking on the embassy's door in 2014.
A 100-metre section opened in August 2017, the final 70 metres on 7 December 2018, and for the
first time in six decades the full 1,100-metre circle was walkable.

Here is the part the reopening headlines did not put first: the stretch that completes the circle
runs between the palace's north wall and the embassy, behind gates at both ends — the final 70
metres as a walkway inside the palace grounds. When those gates are shut — every Monday, and every
evening for much of the year — the loop breaks exactly where it used to.

## One road, four names

What visitors call "the Deoksugung stone wall road" is, in Seoul's own description, four connected
walks. Knowing the names helps, because their rules differ.

| Segment | What it is | Open |
|---|---|---|
| Deoksugung-gil | The classic southern stretch, Daehanmun *(dae-han-mun)* to the Seoul Museum of Art | Always. It is a public street — free, no gate |
| Jeongdong-gil *(jeong-dong-gil)* | Continues from the museum crossing past the old legation quarter | Always. Also a public street |
| The gated walkway | The bypass between the palace's north wall and the British Embassy — 100 m opened in 2017, 70 m in 2018 | Tue–Sun. May–Oct 09:00–20:30, Nov–Apr 09:00–17:30 |
| Gojong's Path *(go-jong-ui gil)* | A 120 m lane from the walkway to Jeongdong Park and the old Russian legation | Tue–Sun. Feb–Oct 09:00–18:00, Nov–Jan 09:00–17:30 |

Hours are the most recent published by Seoul's city portal; sources and dates are at the end.
The palace itself keeps separate hours — 09:00 to 21:00, last entry 20:00, closed Mondays — and
which of Seoul's five palaces is worth your one palace day is
[a separate question we have answered](/en/guides/five-palaces-seoul-which-one/).

One more thing we noticed while checking this, and it is worth knowing before you argue with a
locked gate. The city's three official statements about the walkway's closing time do not agree
with each other. The 2018 opening notice said 09:00 to 18:00. The city portal's 2022 guide says
20:30 in summer, 17:30 in winter. The palace those gates open into now runs to 21:00. Rules have
clearly been revised at least once, so treat the sign posted at the gate as the final word — and
if walking the complete loop matters to you, do it in daylight, on any day that is not Monday.
The southern stretch, the famous one, never closes; only the shortcut through the palace does.

## The wall Goblin walked

Deoksugung Doldamgil is one of the two Seoul locations — the other is Bukchon — that the press
names first when the drama *Goblin* comes up. When the Korea Tourism Organization appointed
Bollywood actress Hina Khan as an honorary ambassador in May 2025, the itinerary reported by
Newsis was exactly that pairing: Bukchon Hanok Village and Deoksugung Doldamgil as *Goblin*
filming locations, then Jumunjin for the BTS music video connection. If you have already made
[the trip to the Goblin breakwater in Gangneung](/en/scenes/goblin-jumunjin-breakwater/), this is
the city half of the same pilgrimage, reachable for a subway fare instead of a KTX ticket.

We have not matched individual scenes to individual metres of wall, and we are not going to
pretend otherwise. What the location offers is simpler: the wall, the canopy, and the same long
walking frame the cameras liked. Stand on the southern stretch, shoot along the stones, and the
picture explains itself.

## Autumn is the argument

Seoul's English travel guide says the path is at its most beautiful in spring and autumn, and in
2022 the city put Deoksugung-gil on its official list of autumn-foliage streets. Our photographs
of the road — supplied by the Korea Tourism Organization — are autumn photographs: a full yellow
canopy over the benches, leaves on the lane. That is the version of this road people travel for.

The window is short. When the colour peaks in Seoul, and how briefly, is covered with Korea
Forest Service data in [our autumn foliage article](/en/now/korea-autumn-foliage-when-and-where/) —
the short version is that November, not October, is when the city's ginkgo turns, and the show
does not wait for a second weekend. Outside autumn the road is still a pleasant, flat, free
half-hour; in autumn it is the reason you got off at City Hall.
