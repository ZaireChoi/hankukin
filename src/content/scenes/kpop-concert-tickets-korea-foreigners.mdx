14:59 on 18 June 2026. That was the moment the fanclub closed for a Seoul fanmeeting that July — and the notice announcing the fanmeeting went up the same day.

So the sequence, for anyone who found out about the show by reading the announcement, was: learn it exists, discover the presale requires fanclub membership, discover membership closed earlier that day. Not sold out. Not outbid. Ineligible before you started.

This is the part that English-language advice tends to skip. It says the tickets go fast, which is true and useless. The tickets going fast is a problem you can prepare for. The four things below are problems you can only prepare for *early*, and two of them cannot be undone once you get them wrong.

## Wall 1 — the Korean site wants an identity you cannot have

Korea's big ticketing sites run a Korean-language page and, sometimes, a separate global page. They are not translations of each other. They are different products with different rules.

To buy on the Korean page you need a verified account, and the verification is the Korean one:

본인인증 *(bon-in-in-jeung)* — "verifying that you are yourself" — is the same wall that stops visitors using Korean banking and delivery apps. A Korean phone number alone does not clear it. Tourist eSIMs hand you an 010 number and then leave you short of exactly this, which is [the subject of its own article here](/en/decode/korea-esim-no-phone-number/).

The global page exists precisely to route around this: you register with your passport name and you are verified against the passport at the venue instead. That is a real solution, and it is why "just use the global page" is the standard advice.

It is also where Wall 3 lives.

## Wall 2 — the presale is not a race, it is a membership list

For most K-pop shows in Korea, the first sale is a fanclub presale, and general sale gets the remainder:

> 일반 예매는 팬클럽 선예매 진행 후 잔여석에 한해 진행되며, 선예매 시 매진 여부에 따라 일반 예매 시 예매 가능한 좌석이 없을 수도 있습니다.
>
> *General sale is conducted only for seats remaining after the fanclub presale, and depending on whether the presale sells out, there may be no seats available at general sale at all.*

That last clause is the operative one. General sale is not a second chance with worse seats. It is a second chance that may contain zero seats.

Getting into the presale is a multi-step qualification, and the steps run backwards from what an outsider would guess:

1. **Be a paying member of the official fanclub already** — with a cutoff that can fall on announcement day.
2. **Verify that membership on the ticketing site**, inside a separate window that closes before the presale starts. One 2026 show required a membership code plus the last four digits of the phone number registered with the fanclub.
3. **Then** compete for tickets, at one ticket per person per show.

Miss step 1 or step 2 and step 3 does not exist for you. There is no queue you can join late.

## Wall 3 — the one-way door

This is the one worth reading twice, because it is invisible and permanent.

If a show sells through both the Korean page and the global page, the fanclub verification you do binds you to whichever page you did it on:

The same notice states that once verification is complete the account cannot be changed and cannot be verified again — and that deleting the account does not restore the ability to re-verify.

Put those together and the failure mode writes itself. An overseas fan lands on the Korean page first, because that is where the artist's own social post pointed, verifies fanclub membership there because the button was right in front of them, and only afterwards discovers they cannot complete a purchase on that page without Korean identity verification. The global page is still there. It is simply no longer available to them for this presale, and there is no support ticket that undoes it.

**So the order of operations matters more than the speed.** Decide which page you are buying on *before* you click any verification button — and if you do not hold Korean identity verification, that decision has already been made for you.

## Wall 4 — the ticket is attached to a person

Korean concert tickets for major shows are not bearer tickets. The notices are blunt about it: identity is checked at the venue by comparing the booking against your ID, and if you are not the person who booked, you do not go in.

Two consequences that catch visitors out:

- **Buying from a reseller is not a fallback.** It is money spent on a door you will be turned away from. One 2026 notice went further and disabled bank-transfer payment for the show outright, stating the reason as preventing scalping and illegal transfer.
- **The name you type is the name that must be on your passport** — same spelling, same order, same capitalisation, same spaces. This is stated for the global page and repeated for the fanclub survey. A middle name dropped for convenience is a real risk at the door.

Wheelchair seating is a separate route again: phone booking only, through a Korean call centre operating Korean business hours, with the buyer's own ID and disability registration card checked at collection and no proxy pickup.

## What you can actually do

Ranked by how much difference it makes, not by how easy it is.

**Join the official fanclub before there is a show to join it for.** This is the whole game. Every other step is downstream of it, and it is the only one that cannot be done quickly once a tour is announced.

**Pick your page first, then verify.** Global page if you do not have Korean identity verification. Do not click a fanclub verification button on the Korean page "just to see".

**Register your account and your name well in advance.** Passport spelling, exactly. Not on sale day.

**Treat general sale as a real possibility, not a consolation.** For a fanmeeting or a smaller venue it often is the only realistic route, and the limit is typically two per person including anything you got in the presale.

**Check which platform this particular artist uses.** Shows are split across several Korean ticketing companies by agency and promoter, and the platform that carried your favourite group's last tour may not carry the next one. The global platform formerly called interpark Global rebranded to NOL World on 1 December 2025 and lists the main Seoul-area venues — KSPO Dome, Jamsil Indoor Stadium, Olympic Hall, Inspire Arena — but "there is a global platform" and "this show is on it" are different claims, and only the second one gets you in. Of the two notices we read for this article, one described a global booking page and one did not mention one at all.

## What this article does not claim

**Every show sets its own rules, and we read two of them.** The prices, dates, purchase limits and verification steps above come from published notices for two specific events — a 2025 arena tour finale and a 2026 fanmeeting. We are describing a pattern that recurs across those notices, not a policy that binds all shows, all artists or all ticketing companies. The notice for your show is the only document that governs your show.

**We did not verify the publication hour of the announcement.** The fanmeeting notice is dated 18 June 2026 and the fanclub membership cutoff was 14:59 on 18 June 2026. We can show that they fell on the same day. We cannot show which came first, and we have not claimed it.

**We did not test any of this by booking.** We read the operators' own notices in Korean and English. We have not run an account through verification, and we cannot tell you what any particular error message means.

**I-PIN is named here because the notice names it.** We did not investigate whether that route is still practically available, or whether any of it is reachable without Korean residency. If you are told a workaround exists, get it from the ticketing company, not from us.

**There is no photograph on this page.** The only Seoul concert-venue images in our library turned out to be the picnic ground at Olympic Park — snow, benches, bare trees, no arena in any of the six frames. A park photograph would have decorated this article without telling you anything, so it is not here.
