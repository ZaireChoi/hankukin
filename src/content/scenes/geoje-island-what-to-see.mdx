There is a reason a lot of Koreans suddenly learned to say **"거제 야호"** this year, and it is worth explaining before we get to the island.

In February 2026 the leader of the girl group RESCENE, **WONYI**, opened a personal YouTube channel. The clips that spread were built around her own accent — she is from **Okpo, on Geoje island**, and she used the local speech rather than smoothing it out. She has called herself the first idol to come from Geoje, and she promoted the place relentlessly. Six months later a song of theirs from 2024 was number one on Melon. [The chart side of that story is here.](/en/scenes/yeokjuhaeng-korean-chart-reverse-running/)

This page is about the island, which was worth visiting before any of that happened.

## Where Geoje actually is

Geoje is **Korea's second-largest island after Jeju**, off the south coast in South Gyeongsang province. It is not remote: it is joined to the mainland by bridge, sits next to Busan, and its economy is shipbuilding rather than tourism — two of the world's largest shipyards are there, one of them at Okpo.

That last fact shapes the visit. **Geoje is a working island, not a resort.** The coastline is spectacular and the towns are industrial, and Korean visitors accept that trade without thinking about it.

## Windy Hill (바람의 언덕)

A bare grass headland at **Dojangpo**, on the south-east of the island, dropping to the sea on three sides.

There is no building on it and no admission. You walk up a short paved path from the village, and the hill does the rest — open grass, wind, and water that is genuinely turquoise on a clear day rather than the grey most Korean coasts offer.

It is the single most photographed spot on Geoje and it takes about twenty minutes. Combine it with the boat to **Oedo Botania**, a garden island, which leaves from the same area.

## Maemiseong (매미성) — the castle one man built

This is the thing on Geoje you will remember, and it is not old.

In 2003, **Typhoon Maemi** — one of the most destructive storms in Korean records — tore through the south coast. A farmer on Geoje lost his crops to the sea, and he began building a stone wall to hold the water back. He kept building. What stands now is a substantial stone castle of terraces, arches and battlements on the shoreline, made by one man over years, without a plan or a construction permit.

It is named after the typhoon that caused it. **Admission is free**, because it is not an attraction — it is a wall that got out of hand and became one.

Go for the story rather than the scale. Standing on it, the interesting thing is not that it looks medieval; it is that every stone was carried there by a person who was, at the start, only trying to protect a field.

## Okpo (옥포) — where Yi Sun-sin won his first battle

Okpo is the shipbuilding town WONYI is from, and it is also one of the most consequential place names in Korean history.

In **1592**, at the start of the Japanese invasion, **Admiral Yi Sun-sin fought his first naval engagement in Okpo bay and won it** — destroying a large part of the Japanese fleet anchored there and losing no ships of his own. It was the first Korean victory of the war, at a point when the land campaign was collapsing, and it began the run of naval victories that Yi is remembered for.

The **Okpo Great Victory Commemorative Park (옥포대첩기념공원)** sits above the bay, with an exhibition hall covering the battle and the ships.

Two things make this worth an hour even if you are not interested in military history. The first is that the bay you are looking at is still full of ships — enormous ones, under construction, in the same water. The second is that Korea's most revered historical figure started here, at a place most visitors have never heard of.

## Getting there

**From Seoul:** the practical route is KTX to **Busan** (about 2h30) and then bus or car across the **Geoga Bridge**, which links Busan to Geoje directly. There are also direct express buses from Seoul, which take considerably longer.

**On the island:** Geoje is large and the sights are spread along the coast. Buses run but they are infrequent between villages. Most Korean visitors drive, and if you are combining Windy Hill, Maemiseong and Okpo in one day, a car or a taxi for the day is the realistic option.

**When:** **late April to early June, and September to October.** The south coast is milder than Seoul in winter and very humid in midsummer — the seasonal reasoning is in [the month-by-month calendar](/en/now/best-time-to-visit-korea-calendar/).

## What this article does not claim

**We have not verified admission, opening hours or transport timetables for any site on this page**, and we have deliberately not invented them. Windy Hill and Maemiseong are open outdoor sites with no admission as far as we can establish; Oedo Botania and the Okpo park charge, and we do not have current figures. Check with Geoje city tourism before you build a schedule.

**The Maemiseong account is the widely reported one.** That a Geoje resident began building the wall after Typhoon Maemi in 2003 and continued for years is the standard telling in Korean media and tourism material. We have not sourced it from a primary document or spoken to anyone involved, and details such as exact dates and dimensions should be treated as approximate.

**The Okpo battle summary is the conventional account** of the first engagement of the Imjin War in 1592. We have not consulted primary records for ship counts, and different sources give different figures; we have therefore described the outcome rather than quoting numbers.

**WONYI's connection to Geoje is a hometown and a stated affection for it.** She is from Okpo and has spoken publicly about promoting the place. Nothing here is a location she endorses commercially, a place she has appeared at for a brand, or a claim about where any video was filmed — and we are not implying any of those.

**We have not been to Geoje.** This is assembled from Korean media reporting and from Korea Tourism Organization photographs published under an open licence.
