The [Standard Korean Dictionary](https://stdict.korean.go.kr/main/main.do) has eighteen separate entries for the syllable 방 *(bang)* — a surname, a constellation, a move in a board game, a way of shaving medicinal rhinoceros horn. Seventeen of them you will never need. The seventh is the one doing the work on every second sign in a Korean entertainment district: 房, "a compartment walled off with walls and the like, for living or working in" — **a room.**

You probably know this word already without knowing you know it. *Noraebang* and *jjimjilbang* have travelled into English whole, as if they were unanalysable Korean institutions. They are not. Each one is an ordinary activity with 방 bolted on the back, and once you see the seam, a whole family of signs unlocks at once. If you can sound out Hangul — [ninety minutes with part one](/en/hangul/learn-to-read-korean-signs-alphabet/) is what it takes — you can read every member of the family on sight.

## The room family

All definitions below are the national dictionary's, not our paraphrases.

| The sign says | Say it | The room where you… |
|---|---|---|
| **노래방** | no-rae-bang | sing — a soundproofed room with an accompaniment machine that puts the lyrics on a screen |
| **찜질방** | jjim-jil-bang | sweat — heated rooms finished in red clay or elvan stone, with rest areas and a sauna attached |
| **피시방** | pi-si-bang | game — computers set up for customers to use the internet |
| **빨래방** | ppal-lae-bang | do laundry — automatic washing machines you operate yourself |
| **만화방** | man-hwa-bang | read — comics rented by the visit, read on the spot |
| **머리방** | meo-ri-bang | get your hair done — the dictionary lists it as a plain synonym of 미용실, the beauty salon |

One row of that table contains a small monument. The dictionary prints the origin of 피시방 as **PC房** — two Latin letters welded directly onto a Chinese character. That hybrid, sitting in the official dictionary of the Korean language, is as compact a history of modern Korea as you will find anywhere.

The entry for 찜질방 is also worth reading in the original, because it is more precise than most guidebooks bother to be:

Note what the definition promises: not just heat, but *rest facilities*. That clause — 휴게 시설 — is the difference between a sauna and a jjimjilbang, and it is why the jjimjilbang belongs on a traveller's list at all. Its older, plainer cousin is the 목욕탕 *(mo-gyok-tang)* — 湯 is the character for hot water — which the dictionary defines simply as a facility for bathing. What a Seoul bathhouse actually costs is a question for [our food-and-services price survey](/en/now/korea-food-prices-what-meals-cost/), which has the surveyed figure.

## The endings that are not rooms

방 is the leisure suffix. The working businesses on the same street end in other syllables, and four of them cover most doors:

- **~소** *(so, 所 — a place)*. 세탁소 *(se-tak-so)* is the laundry **where someone else does it**: the dictionary says it takes money to wash and iron *other people's* washing. 이발소 *(i-bal-so)* is the barbershop, defined as cutting chiefly men's hair.
- **~실** *(sil, 室 — a room, the formal word)*. 미용실 *(mi-yong-sil)*: perms, cuts, make-up and the rest of the beauty trade. The dictionary's synonym list for it runs to four entries, 머리방 among them — the same salon can carry either sign.
- **~탕** *(tang, 湯 — hot water)*. 목욕탕, above. If the sign ends in 탕 and it is not [a soup restaurant](/en/hangul/korean-restaurant-signs-last-word/), it is a bath.
- **~점** *(jeom, 店 — a shop)*. The famous one is 편의점 *(pyeon-ui-jeom)*, the convenience store, and here the dictionary does something wonderful: **the 24 hours are written into the definition itself** — "a general store open twenty-four hours for the customers' convenience." In Korea, round-the-clock is not a marketing promise; lexicographically speaking, it is what the word means.

The odd one out is the optician. The current official word is 안경원 *(an-gyeong-won)*, with 院, a hall — the dictionary defines it as the place where a licensed optician tests your eyes and fits or sells glasses. Its entry for 안경점 *(an-gyeong-jeom)* reads, in full: "the former name of 안경원." The street has not caught up — both versions hang over shopfronts — and either way it is the same ten-minute, no-appointment errand [we covered on its own](/en/now/buy-glasses-in-korea-price-time/).

## Where the dictionary lags the street

Reading definitions against real signs turns up seams, and two are worth flagging.

The entry for 빨래방 defines it by its coin slot — 동전을 넣어, "insert coins" — which tells you when the definition was written. Machines on the street have moved on with payment technology; the word has not needed to. We make no claim about what any particular laundromat accepts today — the honest reading is that 빨래방 means *self-service laundry*, and the coins are a fossil of the machine that named it.

The dictionary also has nothing yet on the newest member of the family you will actually see: 코인노래방, the coin noraebang, a compact walk-in variant of the singing room. What we can tell you about it comes from search data rather than lexicography — its Korean search interest has run flat for two years straight, which, as we argued in [our article on what Koreans actually search for](/en/now/what-koreans-search-for-trends-2026/), is the signature of infrastructure rather than fad. The room words endure; that is rather the point of them.
